Salary Management

A web app that replaces ACME's salary spreadsheets and answers questions about how the company pays people.

AuthorSwapnil Dongre
Date2 October 2026
StatusWritten before implementation
01

Goal

ACME's HR team tracks pay for 10,000 employees across several countries in spreadsheets, which is slow and easy to get wrong. Build web software that lets the HR Manager manage salary data and answer questions about how the organisation pays people.

The user is the HR Manager at ACME, who is allowed to see every employee's pay.

  • Salary data is accurate, stored in one place, and keeps its full history.
  • The HR Manager can answer a question about pay across the company in under a minute, without opening Excel.
02

Scope and features

A  ·  Managing salary data

  • Employee listSearch and filter by country, department, job level and status. Paginated on the server so 10,000 records stay fast.
  • Employee profileJob and personal details, plus the complete salary history for that person.
  • Record a pay changeEnter a new salary with an effective date and a reason, such as promotion or annual review. The previous record is kept, never overwritten.
  • Pay bandsExpected minimum, midpoint and maximum salary for each job level in each country.
  • Multiple currenciesSalaries held in local currency, with the exchange rate stored on each record so company-wide totals are consistent and repeatable.

B  ·  Answering questions about pay

  • DashboardTotal annual payroll cost, headcount, and median pay across the company in one currency.
  • BreakdownsMedian pay and the 25th to 75th percentile range, by country, department and job level.
  • OutliersWho is paid below the minimum or above the maximum of their pay band.
  • Pay equityMedian pay by gender within the same job level and country, hidden for any group under 5 people so no individual's pay can be worked out.
  • CSV exportDownload any filtered view for reports and board decks.

C  ·  Data

  • Seed scriptCreates 10,000 employees with realistic salaries, currencies and change history, including deliberate outliers so the dashboard has something real to show.
03

Deliberately left out

Each item below is a decision, not an oversight. The highlighted row is the one to add first before anyone uses this for real.

Not buildingReasoning
Login, user accounts and permissionsThe brief describes one trusted HR Manager. Auth is well understood and would use up build time without showing design judgment. This is the first thing to add before real use.
Running payroll: payslips, tax, deductionsA separate and heavily regulated system that differs per country. Managing salaries and paying salaries are two different products.
Bonuses, equity and benefitsEach comes with its own vesting and accrual rules. Base salary carries most of the analytical value, and the data model leaves room to add them later.
Approval workflow for pay changesNeeds the multiple users excluded above. Each change already records who made it and why, which is what a workflow would later build on.
Bulk CSV uploadThe hard part is handling bad rows and conflicts, which is a lot of work for little insight. Export is included because HR genuinely needs it.
Live currency exchange ratesReports would give a different answer each time they ran. Rates are stored in the database and saved onto each record instead.
Translating the interfaceThe HR Manager works in English. Currency and date formatting per country is still handled.
Hiring, performance reviews, leaveOutside the problem. The brief is about salary.
